**TL;DR:** Pick up cold and frozen food last and refrigerate it within two hours. Label and date everything, use oldest first, never thaw on the counter, and eat refrigerated leftovers within three to four days. The freezer stops the clock; it does not reset it.

## At the store

- Shop for shelf-stable goods first, chilled and frozen goods last.
- Skip dented-at-the-seam or bulging cans, torn packaging, and cracked eggs.
- Bag raw meat separately from produce.
- Go straight home. On hot days, bring a cooler bag.

## Reading date labels

- **Use by** — a safety date on highly perishable foods. Respect it.
- **Best before** or **best by** — a quality date. The food is usually safe afterward if stored properly, but may be less pleasant.
- **Sell by** — for the store's stock rotation, not for you.

Labels vary by country. When a label and your senses disagree on a perishable food, side with caution.

## Storing food

- **First in, first out** — put new purchases behind older ones.
- **Label and date** leftovers and anything you portion or freeze. Masking tape and a marker never go out of date.
- Keep the fridge at 40°F (4°C) or below, and the freezer at 0°F (-18°C).
- Refrigerate cut fruit, cooked rice, cooked vegetables, and opened jars that say so.
- Store potatoes, onions and garlic somewhere cool, dark and dry — but not together, as onions speed up sprouting.
- Bread keeps better at room temperature or frozen than in the fridge, where it goes stale faster.

## Thawing safely

Never thaw meat, poultry or seafood on the counter. The outside sits in the danger zone while the middle is still frozen.

- **In the refrigerator** — slowest and safest. Allow about a day for every 4 to 5 pounds (about 2 kg) of whole turkey, less for smaller items. Food thawed this way can be refrozen.
- **In cold water** — sealed in a leak-proof bag, submerged, water changed every 30 minutes. Cook immediately after.
- **In the microwave** — cook immediately after, because parts may start to cook.
- **Cooking from frozen** — safe; it just takes about 50% longer.

## Leftovers

- Refrigerate within two hours, in shallow containers.
- Eat within **three to four days**, or freeze.
- Reheat to 165°F (74°C).
- Frozen leftovers stay safe indefinitely at 0°F (-18°C), but quality fades. Most are best within a few months.

## Foods that need special respect

- **Cooked rice** — can carry spores of a toxin-producing bacterium that survive cooking. Cool it quickly, refrigerate promptly, and reheat only once.
- **Garlic or herbs in oil** — homemade versions can support botulism. Keep them refrigerated and use within a few days, or freeze.
- **Home canning** — low-acid foods such as vegetables, meat and beans must be pressure-canned using a tested recipe. Discard any can or jar that bulges, leaks, spurts, or smells off — without tasting.
- **Raw red kidney beans** — contain a natural toxin. Soak and boil hard for at least 10 minutes before slow cooking; a slow cooker alone may not get hot enough.

## When in doubt, throw it out

Mold on soft foods, a sour smell, a slimy texture, or an unknown age are all reasons to discard. Pathogens often give no warning at all, which is why the dates and the clock matter more than your nose.

## Why this outlasts any technology

Packaging, delivery and refrigeration will keep improving. Time, temperature and labeling remain the backbone of safe storage. Tomorrow we pick up a knife.
