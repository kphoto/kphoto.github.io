**TL;DR:** Keep cold food at or below 40°F (4°C) and hot food at or above 140°F (60°C). Food left in between for more than two hours — one hour above 90°F (32°C) — goes in the bin. Cook to safe internal temperatures measured with a thermometer, cool leftovers quickly, and reheat to 165°F (74°C).

## What the danger zone is

The danger zone is the temperature range of roughly **40°F to 140°F (4°C to 60°C)**. Within it, many bacteria can double in about twenty minutes. A few hundred cells on a chicken salad at lunchtime can become millions by dinner. Some bacteria also produce toxins that cooking does not destroy, which is why reheating spoiled food does not make it safe.

## The two-hour rule

- Perishable food should spend **no more than two hours** in the danger zone in total — shopping, prep, serving and leftovers all count.
- If the room or outdoor temperature is above **90°F (32°C)**, the limit is **one hour**.
- When in doubt, throw it out. Smell and appearance are not reliable tests.

## Cold holding

- Refrigerator at **40°F (4°C) or below** — around 37°F (3°C) gives you a margin.
- Freezer at **0°F (-18°C)**. Freezing pauses bacteria; it does not kill them.
- Keep an inexpensive appliance thermometer in both. Built-in dials are often wrong.

## Hot holding

Food kept warm for serving should stay at **140°F (60°C) or above**. A slow cooker on "warm", a low oven, or a chafing dish works. Lukewarm buffets are where trouble starts.

## Safe internal temperatures

Color is not a doneness test — use an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part, away from bone.

- Poultry, whole or ground, and stuffing: **165°F (74°C)**.
- Ground beef, pork, lamb and veal: **160°F (71°C)**.
- Whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb and veal: **145°F (63°C)**, then rest 3 minutes.
- Fish and shellfish: **145°F (63°C)**, or until flesh is opaque and flakes.
- Egg dishes: **160°F (71°C)**.
- Leftovers and casseroles: **165°F (74°C)**.

Check your thermometer occasionally in a glass of ice water stirred for a minute; it should read 32°F (0°C).

## Cooling leftovers fast

A big pot of hot stew cools slowly in the middle and can sit in the danger zone for hours, even inside the fridge.

- Divide into **shallow containers** so the food is no deeper than about two inches (5 cm).
- Cut large roasts into smaller pieces.
- Stir over an ice bath to speed things up.
- Refrigerate within two hours of cooking. Leave lids slightly open until cold, then seal.

The professional standard: from 135°F to 70°F (57°C to 21°C) within two hours, then down to 41°F (5°C) within four more.

## Reheating

Reheat leftovers to **165°F (74°C)** throughout. Bring sauces, soups and gravies to a rolling boil. In a microwave, cover, stir halfway, and let it stand before checking the temperature — microwaves leave cold spots.

## Who needs extra care

Pregnant people, babies and young children, older adults, and anyone with a weakened immune system are more likely to become seriously ill. For them, be strict about unpasteurized milk and juice, undercooked eggs and meat, raw sprouts, and deli meats that have not been reheated until steaming.

## Why this outlasts any technology

Smart fridges and probe thermometers will keep improving, but the biology of bacterial growth will not. Understand the zone and you can judge any situation — a power cut, a picnic, a potluck — without an app. Tomorrow: keeping raw and ready-to-eat food apart.
