**TL;DR:** Most food poisoning at home is carried by hands, not by exotic ingredients. Wash with soap for at least 20 seconds at the right moments, keep sick people out of the kitchen, tie back hair, cover cuts, and use a clean spoon every time you taste. Everything else in this series builds on that.

## Why hygiene comes first

Cooking is applied biology. Bacteria, viruses and parasites ride into food on hands, sneezes, rings and phone screens. You cannot see, smell or taste most of them, and cooking does not always save you — salads, bread, garnishes and leftovers are often eaten without further heat. The cheapest, most effective food safety tool ever invented is soap and running water.

## How to wash your hands properly

1. Wet your hands with clean running water, warm or cold.
2. Lather with soap — backs of hands, between fingers, thumbs, and under the nails.
3. Scrub for at least 20 seconds. Humming a short song twice is the classic timer.
4. Rinse under running water.
5. Dry with a clean towel or paper towel. Damp hands spread germs more easily than dry ones.

Hand sanitizer is a backup, not a replacement. It does not work well on greasy or visibly dirty hands, and it does not remove everything soap does.

## When to wash

- Before you start cooking, and every time you come back to the kitchen.
- After handling raw meat, poultry, seafood, eggs, or unwashed produce.
- After touching the bin, a pet, your phone, your face or hair, money, or cleaning chemicals.
- After using the bathroom, coughing, sneezing, or blowing your nose.
- Between tasks, especially when switching from raw to ready-to-eat food.

If in doubt, wash. It takes less time than reading this sentence twice.

## Cook healthy, or don't cook

If you have vomiting or diarrhea, let someone else cook — and keep it that way for at least 48 hours after symptoms stop. Some of the most contagious stomach viruses spread easily through food and survive well on surfaces. A professional kitchen sends sick staff home; a home kitchen should do the same.

## Small habits that add up

- **Hair** tied back. **Jewelry** off — rings and watches trap food and bacteria.
- **Cuts** covered with a waterproof dressing, ideally a brightly colored one, plus a glove on that hand.
- **Aprons and towels** — keep a towel for hands and a different one for dishes, and change them often.
- **Tasting** — use a clean spoon each time. Never put a tasting spoon back in the pot.
- **Phones** — they go everywhere your hands go. Keep them out of the cooking area or wash after touching them.

## Why this outlasts any technology

Appliances will get smarter and ingredients will be delivered faster, but food will always be prepared by someone's hands, and microbes will always be invisible. A twenty-second habit beats any gadget. Tomorrow we tackle the single most important number in food safety — the danger zone.
