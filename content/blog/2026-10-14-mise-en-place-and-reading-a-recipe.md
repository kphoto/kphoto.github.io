**TL;DR:** Read the whole recipe before you start, gather and prepare every ingredient and tool, then cook. Measure by weight when precision matters, preheat on purpose, keep raw and ready-to-eat items separated, and clean as you go. Calm cooking is safe cooking.

## What mise en place means

_Mise en place_ is French for "putting in place". In professional kitchens it means every ingredient is washed, measured and cut, and every tool is within reach, before the first pan is heated. At home it means you never stop stirring to chop an onion while the garlic burns.

## Read the recipe twice

The first read tells you what you are making. The second read tells you what will catch you out:

- **Hidden time** — marinating overnight, chilling dough, bringing butter to room temperature, soaking beans.
- **Hidden steps** — "1 onion, diced" means dicing comes before the cooking starts.
- **Equipment** — a specific pan size, a thermometer, a blender.
- **Order** — what goes in when, and what can be prepared in advance.

If a recipe uses a term you don't know, look it up before the stove is on.

## Measuring

- **Weight beats volume** for baking. A cup of flour can vary greatly depending on how it is scooped; 125 grams is always 125 grams. A kitchen scale is one of the best inexpensive tools you can own.
- Spoon flour into a cup and level it if you must use volume. Don't pack it unless told to.
- Liquid measures are read at eye level.
- Cooking forgives; baking is chemistry and rewards precision.

## Set up your station

1. Clear and clean the counter.
2. Wash your hands.
3. Lay out a board with a damp towel under it, a bowl for scraps, and a towel for wiping.
4. Prepare ingredients into small bowls or piles, grouped by when they are added.
5. Keep raw proteins in their own area, on their own board, prepared last.
6. Preheat the oven or pan at the right moment — not so early it scorches, not so late you wait.

## Preheating matters

Food added to a cold pan steams and sticks instead of searing. A properly preheated oven gives baked goods the burst of heat they need to rise. Let the pan heat until oil shimmers before adding food for a sear, and wait for the oven to reach temperature — an oven thermometer reveals how accurate your dial is.

## Think in time, not just steps

Work backwards from when you want to eat. Start with whatever takes longest. Long-simmering sauces, rice and roasts go first; quick vegetables and garnishes go last. Write the timeline down for big meals.

## Taste and adjust

Recipes are written for an average stove, average ingredients and an average palate. Yours will differ. Taste at each stage with a clean spoon, and trust what your senses tell you about doneness alongside the clock.

## Why this outlasts any technology

Recipe apps, voice assistants and smart ovens will change how instructions reach you. Reading them carefully, preparing before you begin, and organizing your space will always be what separates chaos from control. Tomorrow: how heat actually cooks food.
