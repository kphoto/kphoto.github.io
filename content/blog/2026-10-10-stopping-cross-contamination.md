**TL;DR:** Keep raw meat, poultry, seafood and eggs away from anything that will not be cooked again. Use separate boards or a strict order of work, store raw meat on the bottom shelf, don't rinse raw chicken, and wash hands and tools between tasks. Allergens follow the same paths, but heat does not remove them.

## How contamination travels

Cross-contamination is the transfer of harmful microbes from one food or surface to another. The usual routes:

- **Food to food** — raw chicken drips onto lettuce in the fridge.
- **Surface to food** — a board used for raw fish is then used for slicing bread.
- **Hands to food** — touching raw burger patties, then the bun.
- **Tools to food** — tongs used to place raw meat on the grill are used to serve the cooked meat.

The danger is greatest with ready-to-eat foods: salads, fruit, bread, cheese, cooked leftovers. Nothing is going to cook the germs off them later.

## Practical separation

- **Boards** — use one for raw meat, poultry and seafood, and another for produce and ready-to-eat food. Color-coding helps, but any system you follow every time works.
- **Order of work** — if you only have one board, prepare vegetables and ready-to-eat foods first, raw proteins last, then wash everything.
- **Plates and tongs** — never put cooked food back on a plate that held raw food. Grab a clean one.
- **Marinades** — marinate in the fridge. If you want to use some as a sauce, set it aside before the raw meat goes in, or boil used marinade before serving.
- **Shopping** — bag raw meat separately, and keep it from leaking onto other groceries.

## The refrigerator hierarchy

Store food so that anything that might drip only lands on something that will be cooked hotter:

1. Top: ready-to-eat foods, leftovers, dairy.
2. Middle: produce and items that will be cooked gently.
3. Bottom: raw meat, poultry and seafood, in a tray or sealed container.

## Don't rinse raw poultry

Washing raw chicken or turkey sprays bacteria across the sink, counter and nearby food — up to an arm's length away. It does not make the meat safer; cooking to 165°F (74°C) does. Pat it dry with paper towels if needed, then wash your hands.

## Produce

Rinse fruits and vegetables under running water just before using them, even ones you will peel — a knife carries surface dirt into the flesh. Scrub firm produce such as melons and potatoes with a brush. Skip soap and bleach; plain water is the recommendation. Pre-washed bagged greens do not need rewashing.

## Allergen cross-contact

For someone with a food allergy, a trace can cause a severe reaction. Unlike bacteria, **allergen proteins are not destroyed by cooking**.

- Ask about allergies before you cook for others, and take every answer seriously.
- Prepare the allergen-free dish first, with clean tools, and cover it.
- Wash boards, knives and hands with soap and water. Wiping is not enough.
- Watch shared oil, toasters, butter, and serving spoons.
- Read every label, every time. Recipes change.
- If you are not sure a dish is safe, say so. Honesty protects people.

## Why this outlasts any technology

Kitchens will gain new surfaces and gadgets, but contamination will always move the same way: by touch, drip and splash. Learn to see those paths and you can work safely in any kitchen. Tomorrow: the difference between cleaning and sanitizing.
