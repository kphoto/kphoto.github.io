**TL;DR:** Never leave cooking unattended, never put water on a grease fire, turn pan handles inward, and cool burns under running water. Keep a fire extinguisher and know how to use it. Then make the series a habit — wash, separate, cook, chill, clean — every single time.

## Fire

Cooking is a leading cause of home fires, and unattended cooking is the most common culprit.

- **Stay with it.** If you leave the kitchen, turn the heat off.
- Keep towels, paper, oven mitts and curtains away from burners.
- Wear close-fitting sleeves. Loose cuffs catch flames.
- Clean grease buildup from the stove, oven and hood regularly.

## If there's a grease fire

- **Never use water.** It turns instantly to steam and throws burning fat across the room.
- Turn off the heat.
- Slide a lid or a baking sheet over the pan and leave it there until fully cool.
- For a very small fire, baking soda or salt can smother it. Never flour — it can ignite.
- For an oven or microwave fire, keep the door closed and turn it off.
- If the fire isn't out in seconds or is spreading, **get everyone out and call emergency services**.

Keep a fire extinguisher rated for kitchen fires within reach, outside the immediate cooking area, and read its instructions before you need them.

## Burns and scalds

- Turn pot handles inward so they can't be knocked or grabbed by children.
- Use dry oven mitts. Wet cloth conducts heat straight to your hand.
- Lift lids away from you so steam escapes in the other direction.
- Lower food into hot oil gently, away from your body. Dry it first — water makes oil spit.

**First aid:** cool a burn under cool running water for 20 minutes. No ice, butter, or ointments. Remove rings and tight items before swelling starts. Cover loosely with clean plastic wrap or a clean, non-fluffy dressing. Seek medical care for large, deep, or blistering burns, burns to the face, hands or genitals, chemical or electrical burns, and any burn on a child or older adult.

## Slips, children and electricity

- Wipe up spills from the floor immediately.
- Keep children out of the cooking zone or assign them safe, supervised jobs.
- Keep cords away from water and hot surfaces, and dry your hands before touching plugs.
- Store heavy items low and use a sturdy step, not a chair.

## The habits that tie it all together

If you remember nothing else from this series, remember these five words:

1. **Clean** — hands, surfaces and tools, often and properly.
2. **Separate** — raw away from ready-to-eat; allergens away from everyone else's food.
3. **Cook** — to safe internal temperatures, checked with a thermometer.
4. **Chill** — promptly, and never let food linger in the danger zone.
5. **Respect** — the knife, the flame, the hot oil, and the people you feed.

## Becoming a better cook

- Cook the same dish several times. Repetition teaches more than variety.
- Take notes — what you changed, and what happened.
- Learn a few reliable techniques rather than many recipes. Techniques transfer; recipes don't.
- Cook for other people. Their reactions are honest feedback.
- Make mistakes on purpose sometimes. Overbrown something to see where the line is.

## Why this outlasts any technology

Tools will change, delivery will get faster, and machines will cook more of our food. But anyone who understands hygiene, temperature, contamination, heat and flavor will always be able to feed themselves and the people they love — safely and well. Thank you for following Kitchen Fundamentals.
