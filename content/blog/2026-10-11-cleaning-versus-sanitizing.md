**TL;DR:** Clean first with hot soapy water, then sanitize surfaces that touch food. Sanitizers work only on surfaces that are already clean, and only if given contact time. Replace sponges often, swap towels daily, clean as you go, and never mix cleaning chemicals.

## Two different jobs

- **Cleaning** removes food residue, grease and dirt using soap, water and friction. It also removes many germs along the way.
- **Sanitizing** reduces the remaining microbes to safe levels, using heat or a chemical.

The order matters. Grease and crumbs shield bacteria and neutralize many sanitizers. Clean, rinse, then sanitize.

## A simple routine for food-contact surfaces

1. Scrape off debris.
2. Wash with hot soapy water.
3. Rinse with clean water.
4. Sanitize — a dishwasher's hot cycle, a commercial kitchen sanitizer used according to its label, or a dilute bleach solution.
5. Air-dry. Towels can put germs straight back.

A common home bleach solution is **1 tablespoon of unscented household bleach per gallon of water** (about 15 ml per 4 liters). Leave it on for a few minutes before air-drying. Make it fresh, since it weakens over time. Check the label of your product; strengths vary.

## Never mix chemicals

Bleach combined with ammonia or with acids, including some toilet cleaners and vinegar, can release toxic gases. Use one product at a time, rinse between them, keep the room ventilated, and store chemicals away from food, in their original labeled containers.

## Sponges, cloths and towels

The kitchen sponge is often the dirtiest object in the house. It stays damp, warm and full of food.

- Squeeze it out and let it dry between uses.
- Replace it often — weekly is a reasonable habit.
- Use cloths you can launder hot, and swap them daily.
- Keep separate towels for hands, dishes and spills.

## Clean as you go

Professional cooks wipe their station constantly. Fill the sink with hot soapy water before you start, drop tools in as you finish with them, and wipe spills immediately. You end the meal with a manageable kitchen, and raw juices never get the chance to spread.

## High-touch spots people forget

Fridge handles, tap handles, knobs on the stove, light switches, the microwave keypad, can openers, and the rim of the bin. Clean them regularly — they are touched with raw-meat hands more often than you think.

## The refrigerator

- Wipe spills when they happen, especially raw meat juices.
- Every week, check dates and throw out anything past its time.
- Clean the shelves and drawers with warm soapy water periodically.
- Do not overcrowd it — cold air needs to circulate.

## Pests

Insects and rodents carry disease. Store dry goods in sealed containers, take out the trash often, fix leaks, keep crumbs off the floor, and seal gaps. If you see droppings, discard affected food and clean and sanitize thoroughly.

## Why this outlasts any technology

Self-cleaning surfaces and antimicrobial coatings will come and go. Soap, friction, contact time and dry surfaces will still be how a kitchen gets clean. Tomorrow: shopping, storing, thawing and leftovers.
